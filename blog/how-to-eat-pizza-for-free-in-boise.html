How to eat pizza for free in Boise

The complete, slightly unhinged playbook to Flying Pie's "It's Your Day" make-your-own game, and every other way to score a free pie in the valley.

🍕 By The Crust Gazette·Updated June 2026·11 min read

The 20-second version

Almost every day, Flying Pie hands a few lucky people the keys to the kitchen and lets them build a 10-inch pizza for free. It hinges on a "Name of the Day." If the day's name fits you, you bring a valid ID, show up during the 2 to 4 PM or 8 to 10 PM window, and make your own pie at no cost. Below: how the game works, how to boost your odds, and four other ways to get free pizza in the Treasure Valley.

Most "free pizza" offers come with a catch buried in the fine print. Buy two, get a third. Spend forty dollars, get a side. Sign up for emails you'll spend the next year unsubscribing from. Flying Pie does something stranger and a lot more fun. It lets you walk behind the counter, tie on an apron, and build a pizza with your own two hands, for free, just because the calendar says it's your day.

It's called It's Your Day, it has been running since 2002, and it remains one of the most genuinely charming things any pizzeria in Boise does. This guide breaks down exactly how it works, the rules nobody reads until they're standing at the counter, and a few honest tactics to improve your chances. Then we'll cover the other ways to score a free pie across all 7 Flying Pie locations.

What "It's Your Day" actually is

Here's the premise in one breath. Almost every day, Flying Pie picks a theme, a "Name of the Day." If your name fits that theme and there's room on the shift, you get to step into the kitchen and make your own 10-inch specialty pizza, or a 10-inch custom pie with up to five toppings, at no charge. A friendly crew member walks you through it. You stretch the dough, ladle the Whirled-Famous Red Sauce, pile on the toppings, and they bake it for you.

No coupon. No minimum purchase. No loyalty points to redeem. The only currency is having the right name on the right day and showing up ready to play.

You are a guest in the kitchen, not a customer at the counter. That distinction is the whole magic of it.

The official rules, in plain English

Flying Pie keeps the rules loose and good-humored, but they do matter, since this all happens in a working kitchen during real service. Here's what to know before you go:

  • Bring a valid ID. You'll need it when you order in the store or come in to pick up your pie. The name is the whole point, so they check.
  • There are set windows. Kitchen pizza-making happens during specific times, not whenever you wander in. Plan around them (more on the exact hours below).
  • It's a privilege, not an entitlement. You're a guest. Do what the crew asks, when they ask it. They're running a kitchen and looking out for you.
  • Behave, or you're out. Act up or get disrespectful and they can, and will, ask you to leave. Be cool.
  • Dress for a kitchen. No open-toe shoes. Long hair pulled back. This is a food-safety thing, not a fashion note.
  • Bring one wingman. You can bring a single friend or family member into the kitchen with you. Pick wisely.

⏰ The windows that matter

The make-your-own shifts run from 2 to 4 PM and 8 to 10 PM. Each shift can safely fit somewhere between 5 and 20 people, so the earlier in the window you arrive, the better your shot at a spot.

Five honest tips to boost your odds

There's no gaming a calendar, but there's a real difference between people who show up hopeful and people who show up ready. Do these:

  1. Watch the schedule like a hawk. Names are posted ahead of time. Set a reminder for any day that matches yours, a nickname, or a shared family name.
  2. Arrive at the front of the window. With 5 to 20 spots per shift, 2:00 PM beats 3:45 PM and 8:00 PM beats 9:50 PM. Early birds make the pie.
  3. Have your ID out and ready. Fumbling for it slows the line. Smooth and prepared keeps things moving for everyone.
  4. Bring the right wingman. You get one. Bring someone who'll have fun with it, not someone who'll stand in the corner on their phone.
  5. Be genuinely kind to the crew. They're hosting you mid-service. Good energy is remembered, and it makes the whole thing better.

Where it all happens

Flying Pie runs 7 hot spots around the Treasure Valley, so wherever you are, there's a kitchen within reach. Four sit in Boise: Fairview, State Street, Broadway and Overland. Then there's Meridian on South Main, Eagle on South Eagle Road, and Nampa, which also happens to have a 28-tap self-serve beer wall if you'd like something cold with your free pie.
